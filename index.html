The Realities of Scaling (Testable Models)

Clean math fails in real operations. Here is how I am modeling the *actual* constraints of scaling the AU model, moving beyond theoretical formulas to factor in intake dilution and operational chaos.

Selection Quality vs. Ops Load Model

If we scale intake from 50 to 500, we pull from deeper in the talent pool. As baseline capability drops, the time required to drag a student to a "25 LPA outcome" does not scale linearly—it compounds.

The Strategic Imperative: We cannot simply hire 10x mentors for 10x students. Because of dilution, 10x students might require 15x mentors unless we inject automated unblocking before the 1:1 session.

The "Chaos Coefficient" in 1:1 Operations Model

Theoretical math: 250 students × 2 sessions × 1 hr = 500 hrs/month.
Reality: Uneven demand clustering (weekend peaks), 10% no-show/reschedule friction, and sessions overrunning because a bug takes 15 mins to context-switch into.

Peak Clustering: Mentors sit idle on Tuesday morning, but are 150% overbooked on Sunday night before deadlines.
Context Switching: Mentors spend the first 10-15 mins of a 45 min call just understanding where the student's code is failing.

Where My Model Could Be Wrong

A plan without acknowledged weaknesses is just marketing. Here are the specific second-order risks I would need to test immediately if hired.

⚠ The "Premium Illusion" Trap

My hypothesis assumes we can convert some 1:1 sessions into async Loom reviews to save time.

Where it breaks: Students aren't just paying for the technical unblocking; they are paying for the psychological safety of a live senior engineer looking at their code. If async reviews make the product feel "cheap," our NPS will tank, which will spike top-of-funnel acquisition costs, wiping out any ops savings we gained.

⚠ Dilution Outpacing Telemetry

I proposed building telemetry to flag users falling behind to enable targeted intervention.

Where it breaks: If we scale from 50 to 500, the intake baseline drops. Telemetry might reveal that 60% of the cohort is suddenly "At-Risk", triggering a flood of alerts that overruns the mentor team anyway. Telemetry only works if the baseline curriculum can still autonomously support the median user.

Execution: Week 1

Before writing a line of code or deploying a single automation, my first week is dedicated entirely to validating assumptions with real operational data.

Step 01

Quantify the Chaos

Export the last 3 months of mentor calendar data. Calculate the exact "No-show rate," "Reschedule rate," and "Weekend Peak Load." Clean math is useless; I need the actual Ops Utilization %.

Step 02

Audit the 1:1 Logs

Shadow 5 mentor calls and read 100 session notes. Categorize the friction: Is the mentor spending time on syntax? Logic structure? Or career anxiety? We only build automation for the highest-friction category.

Step 03

Ship Internal Dash V0

Using Metabase or a simple internal script, build a single view for the Founders: A list of students whose GitHub/LMS commit velocity has dropped >30% this week. Test if this signal correlates with actual drop-off.
